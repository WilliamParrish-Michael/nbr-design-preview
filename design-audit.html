The pattern

The category runs on one formula

Across every site — luxury or volume — the same structural moves repeat. These aren't style preferences; they're conversion conventions this audience already expects.

7/7

Phone lives in the nav

Every site keeps a tappable phone number in the header, usually in a contrasting colored block. In this category the conversion is the call.

Cool + warm

Calm base, warm CTA

A cool, trustworthy base (teal, navy, sage) carries the page; a single warm accent (gold, bronze, orange, peach) is reserved for the call-to-action.

5/7

Square buttons, not pills

Most CTAs use 0–4px corners. The fully-rounded SaaS "pill" is rare here — sharper corners read more clinical and editorial.

Serif

Serif display = premium

The luxury tier signals status with a serif display face (Meadows, Alta Mira). Volume brands stay all-sans for speed and neutrality.

Full-bleed

Cinematic hero + short line

A full-width image or video, one short empathetic headline, phone in reach. All Points North uses a mountain video — the premium move.

Human

Empathy, not features

Headlines speak to the moment ("Help starts with a conversation"), not the service list. Accreditation badges sit close by for trust.

Mobile

On a phone, it's all about the call

Read from each site's live CSS and DOM at their mobile breakpoints (no rendering needed). The mobile playbook is even more phone-focused than desktop.

7/7

Hamburger / drawer nav

Every site collapses the menu on mobile, and early — most switch by 1024–1199px, not 768. Caron uses a multi-level drawer with a back button; Alta Mira an off-canvas panel.

Sticky call

A floating call button

All Points North, The Meadows, Sierra Tucson and Hazelden show a fixed call button/bar that appears only on phones. It's the defining mobile conversion pattern — and the one New Beginnings is missing.

×7–24

Phone, everywhere

The number repeats across header, drawer, sticky bar and footer — APN has 24 tel links, Caron and Alta Mira 14, AAC 12. On mobile the phone is never more than a thumb away.

What New Beginnings should add: a mobile sticky call bar

The site already does the fundamentals right — it collapses to a hamburger at ~992px and keeps a gold phone button in the header (verified live at 390px, no overflow). The one clear gap versus the field is the persistent, phone-only call action the leaders pin to the screen.

Recommendation: a fixed bottom bar on ≤768px with two thumb-sized targets — Call 24/7 and Verify insurance — visible on every page. It's the single highest-impact mobile change, it matches the sticky-action-bar direction already used elsewhere in your work, and it mirrors exactly what APN, Meadows, Sierra Tucson and Hazelden do to convert mobile traffic.

Checklist

What to do — and what to skip

Do

  • Keep the phone in the nav, tappable, in a contrasting block — every peer does.
  • Cool calm base + one warm CTA color. Reserve the warm accent for the call/verify buttons only.
  • Use a serif display face to signal premium (Fraunces/Cormorant). It's the luxury tier's tell.
  • Lead with a full-bleed hero and one short, empathetic line. Consider a subtle video like APN.
  • Put accreditation near the hero (DHCS, Joint Commission, LegitScript) — real trust marks.
  • Make "Verify insurance" a headline CTA, not buried — Caron and Hazelden both elevate it.

Don't

  • Default to Inter + generic blue. It reads commodity; the volume brands look cheaper for it.
  • Use fully-rounded SaaS pill buttons. The category leans square/4px — sharper is more clinical.
  • Lean on gimmicks (custom cursors, heavy parallax, gradient orbs). None of the leaders do; they read as calm.
  • Fabricate testimonials or stats. FTC + HIPAA exposure; use real, released quotes and real badges.
  • Dump features in the hero. Speak to the moment first; the service list can wait.
  • Put low-contrast text on cream/gold. Keep AA contrast — a real risk in the luxury palette.
