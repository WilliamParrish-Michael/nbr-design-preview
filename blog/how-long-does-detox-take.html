How Long Does Detox Take for Alcohol, Opioids, and Benzos?

January 18, 2026

Typical Detox Timelines, Ranges, and What Can Change Them

One of the most common questions people ask when considering detox is simple but important: how long does detox take?

The honest answer is that detox timelines vary by substance, individual health, and level of medical support. Alcohol detox does not follow the same timeline as opioid detox, and benzodiazepine detox is often much longer and more gradual than people expect.

Understanding realistic detox timelines—by substance—can help reduce uncertainty and support safer planning. This guide breaks down typical detox durations for alcohol, opioids, and benzodiazepines, explains why timelines vary, and outlines what factors can shorten or extend the process.

What “Detox” Actually Refers To

Detox refers to the withdrawal and stabilization phase that occurs after stopping alcohol or drugs. During detox, the body clears substances from the system and adjusts to functioning without them.

Detox focuses on:

  • Managing withdrawal symptoms
  • Preventing medical complications
  • Stabilizing physical health

It does not address the psychological or behavioral aspects of addiction, which are treated in subsequent phases of care.

Why Detox Timelines Vary So Much

There is no universal detox timeline. Duration depends on several variables, including:

  • The substance used
  • Length and intensity of use
  • Previous detox or withdrawal history
  • Overall physical and mental health
  • Whether detox is medically managed

For this reason, detox timelines are best understood as ranges, not fixed endpoints.

How Long Does Alcohol Detox Take?

Alcohol withdrawal typically begins 6–12 hours after the last drink and progresses through several stages.

Typical Alcohol Detox Timeline

  • 6–12 hours: Early symptoms begin
  • 24–72 hours: Symptoms peak
  • 3–7 days: Acute symptoms usually subside

For many people, the most intense phase of alcohol detox lasts 3–5 days, though lingering symptoms such as sleep disruption or anxiety may persist longer.

Factors That Can Extend Alcohol Detox

  • Long-term or heavy alcohol use
  • Prior withdrawal episodes
  • History of seizures or delirium tremens
  • Co-occurring medical conditions

Because alcohol withdrawal can be life-threatening, medical supervision significantly impacts both safety and comfort during this period.

How Long Does Opioid Detox Take?

Opioid detox timelines vary depending on the specific opioid used (short-acting vs long-acting).

Short-Acting Opioids (e.g., heroin, oxycodone)

  • 8–24 hours: Symptoms begin
  • 2–4 days: Symptoms peak
  • 5–7 days: Acute symptoms typically improve

Long-Acting Opioids (e.g., methadone)

  • 24–48 hours: Symptoms begin
  • 3–6 days: Symptoms peak
  • 10+ days: Symptoms gradually improve

While opioid withdrawal is rarely life-threatening, it can be extremely uncomfortable and may increase relapse risk without proper support.

How Long Does Benzodiazepine Detox Take?

Benzodiazepine (benzo) detox is often the longest and most gradual of the three.

Typical Benzo Detox Timeline

  • Initial symptoms may begin days to weeks after dose reduction
  • Detox often involves slow tapering, not abrupt cessation
  • The process can take weeks to months, depending on dose and duration of use

Abruptly stopping benzodiazepines can be dangerous and increase the risk of seizures, which is why benzo detox is usually managed through a carefully structured taper under medical supervision.

What Can Shorten or Lengthen Detox?

Substance Type and Half-Life

Longer-acting substances generally produce longer withdrawal timelines.

Duration and Intensity of Use

Heavier, longer use often leads to more prolonged detox.

Previous Withdrawal Attempts

Repeated withdrawals can increase sensitivity and extend symptoms.

Medical Supervision

Medically managed detox can reduce severity, prevent complications, and provide structured support.

Individual Health Factors

Age, nutrition, mental health, and chronic conditions all influence detox duration.

Why Medically Managed Detox Changes the Experience

Medical detox does not eliminate withdrawal—but it changes how withdrawal is managed.

Medically managed detox may include:

  • Symptom monitoring
  • Medication when appropriate
  • Hydration and nutritional support
  • Clear escalation protocols

This approach often leads to a safer and more predictable detox process, even when timelines remain similar.

Detox Timelines vs Recovery Timelines

It’s important to separate detox duration from recovery duration.

Detox focuses on physical stabilization. Recovery continues well beyond detox and involves:

  • Therapy or counseling
  • Behavioral change
  • Relapse prevention planning
  • Ongoing support

Completing detox does not mean treatment is finished.

Why Expectations Matter Before Detox

Misunderstanding detox timelines can lead to:

  • Frustration
  • Premature dropout
  • Increased relapse risk

Clear expectations help individuals plan appropriately and seek the right level of care.

When Detox Takes Longer Than Expected

Extended detox timelines are not a failure. They often reflect:

  • Medical complexity
  • Necessary tapering
  • Individual safety needs

Adjusting the timeline is sometimes the safest choice.

Learning What Your Detox Timeline Might Look Like

Because detox timelines vary so widely, the most accurate information comes from individual assessment, not averages alone.

Trusted medical resources such as MedlinePlus provide general guidance on detox and medication-assisted approaches, but personal factors matter most.
